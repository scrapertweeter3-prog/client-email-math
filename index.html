An email lookup is a line item nobody quotes

Fixed-price first jobs hide unpaid hours, and the best hidden one is spent hunting a prospect's email address. The lookup happens before the pitch, bills nothing, and its cost is set entirely by the method used to do it.

Put a loaded $25 an hour on the time. Hand-searching and verifying runs about ten good addresses an hour, $2.50 each. A lookup tool that clears 30 an hour cuts the labor to 83 cents, and a $49 monthly subscription spread across 100 addresses adds 49 cents, $1.32 all-in. Two thirds off the labor, roughly half off once the subscription is paid.

Say one in twenty verified addresses turns into a booked call. Hand-searched, every booking carries $50 of lookup labor. Through the tool, $26.47. Four bookings a month makes that $200 against $115.67, with the hours falling from eight to under three.

The rate on the first job shows the bill. A $750 project with 25 hours of work quotes at $30 an hour. Eight hours of manual hunting pulls the real rate to $22.73. Two hours and forty minutes pulls it to $27.11. Clients are paying for the work, so prospecting hours come straight out of the rate.

Price the lookup once and the ranking writes itself: dollars per verified address, then hours back per month, both inherited by every quote that follows.

ExtraHustles prices side-hustle steps by what they do to the real rate, receipts first. The true hourly rate ledger runs through account churning, return pallets, and the cost of finding a client's email address.
